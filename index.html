Stock Pitch · November 2026

JPMorgan Chase & Co. (NYSE: JPM)

BUY

The largest and most diversified bank in the U.S., with the balance sheet to keep winning in good markets and bad.

By Lucas Vitale · Finance, Tulane University A.B. Freeman School of Business

The Pitch in 30 Seconds

I'm recommending a Buy on JPMorgan Chase. JPM makes money across consumer banking, investment banking, trading, and wealth management, so no single business has to carry it. Its capital position lets it grow when competitors pull back, and it keeps out-investing peers in technology.

The main risks are a recession driving up loan losses, falling interest rates squeezing lending margins, and a valuation that already sits above other big banks.

Company Overview

JPMorgan Chase is the largest bank in the United States by total assets. It runs three main businesses:

Consumer & Community Banking

Chase branches, checking and savings accounts, credit cards, mortgages, and auto loans.

Commercial & Investment Bank

M&A advisory, debt and equity underwriting, trading, payments, and lending to businesses of all sizes.

Asset & Wealth Management

Investment management for institutions and individuals, including J.P. Morgan Private Bank.

Investment Thesis

1

Diversified Earnings

When dealmaking slows, consumer banking and payments keep earning. When markets get volatile, trading can pick up the slack. Few banks have that kind of balance.

2

Fortress Balance Sheet

Strong capital and liquidity let JPM play offense in downturns. In 2023 it acquired First Republic Bank during the regional banking crisis, adding deposits and wealthy clients.

3

Scale in Technology

JPM has one of the largest technology budgets in banking, investing in digital banking, payments, and AI. Its scale spreads those costs across far more customers than smaller rivals can.

Key Risks

  • Recession: more borrowers fall behind on loans and credit cards, raising credit losses.
  • Interest rates: rate cuts can shrink net interest income, the spread between what JPM earns on loans and pays on deposits.
  • Regulation: stricter capital rules could limit buybacks and dividends.
  • Valuation: JPM already trades at a premium to peers, so a lot of good news may be priced in.
  • Leadership succession: an eventual CEO transition creates uncertainty for investors.
